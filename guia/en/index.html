New career: AI forward deployed engineer

Companies are buying AI faster than they can make it work. The person who joins a company, finds the real problem, makes AI work, and proves it with numbers has turned it into a profession—and the biggest AI companies are already hiring: official listings publish US$ 135K to US$ 415K a year, and there are already open roles based in Brazil. In short

AI forward deployed engineer (FDE) is the professional an AI company sends to the customer’s workplace: they find the real problem, build a solution with the team, prove it works with a measured test, and get people to use it. This guide explains the role, its potential, and who can follow this path. The free kit on GitHub is your training: a test to find out what you’re missing, a learning path, a simulated customer to practice with, templates, an example of proof that runs on your computer, and interview practice. What it is

The person who makes AI really work inside a company

Instead of handing over a password and wishing you luck, the AI company sends an engineer to sit beside the customer. They don’t sell hours or software: they deliver a result.

😩 Why this role exists

A company buys powerful AI and, months later, nothing works. The AI doesn’t connect to the old system, the data is messy, no one knows what to ask for, and the team goes back to spreadsheets.

The problem isn’t the AI. The company is missing someone who can connect its messy systems to what AI can do.

🧭 What an AI forward deployed engineer does

  • Finds the real problem (the customer asks for “a chatbot”; the problem is usually something else).
  • Builds alongside the customer, using their systems and data.
  • Proves it with a measured test, not a polished demo.
  • Gets the team using it, then comes back to measure results.

The rare part is having both halves

🛠️ The builder

Makes things work: code (with AI writing it), connections between systems, AI agents, and the test that proves the result.

+

🤝 The person the customer trusts

Finds the right problem, agrees on what “done” means, presents to leadership, and gets the team to adopt the tool.

Engineers who enjoy working with people are rare. Consultants who build are rare too. Companies compete for people who have both halves, which is why the training starts by finding out which half you’re missing.

Who can do it

You don’t need to be born a programmer

Job listings ask for Python, but no one expects you to type everything by hand: Claude Code and Codex write the code. You’re expected to read it, ask for the right change, and prove it works. People who often succeed include:

💻 A programmer who likes people

You already build. You need to practice discovery, scoping, and talking with leadership.

📊 A consultant, analyst, or sales engineer who learned to build

You already talk with customers. You need to build things that hold up in real use and prove them with tests.

🚀 A founder, freelancer, or agency owner

You already do this work without the title: you join the customer, find the problem, build, and deliver.

⚠️ It’s not for everyone

Many roles involve frequent travel to customers, a few years of customer experience, and conversations in English. Things get messy: old systems, missing data, people who resist change. If you like building, enjoy working with people, and aren’t put off by a little chaos, this is a great opportunity. It’s also harder to turn into a passing fad than “prompt engineer,” because it takes more than one skill: it’s the whole cycle of finding the problem, building, and proving the result.

The week

A typical week with the customer

Much of the time is spent with the customer, alongside the people doing the work. The rhythm often looks like this:

WednesdayBuild: connect the agent to the customer’s systems and data.The first version running on real data.

FridayDemo for decision-makers + notes for the AI company’s product team: where the model failed and what customers keep asking for.Approval, the next step, and what becomes a reusable recipe.

The proof

Why a demo doesn’t prove it but eval does

The kit includes a support email sorter that puts each message into one of 5 queues (billing, access, delivery, cancellation, other). We ran the kit’s eval on it for real on Nov 20, 2026:

  1. Version 1 passed the demo.
    The obvious examples worked. On an eval with 30 sample emails, written like the ones a support team receives, it got 18 right. It missed “I CAN’T LOG IN,” “I canceled but I’m still being charged,” and 10 others.
  2. We fixed each failure and got version 2.
    30 out of 30. The eval showed 12 cases fixed, none broken.
  3. Then we ran 10 new emails that hadn’t been used to make fixes.
    Version 2 got only 4 right, the same as version 1. It memorized the cases instead of learning the rule.
18/30version 1 on known cases
30/30version 2 on known cases
4/10version 2 on new cases

This is exactly what the customer needs to see and what no demo shows: where it works, where it fails, and whether the improvement is real. The example uses simple rules, with no AI, so it runs on any computer; the kit shows how to swap in your automation or agent.

The interview

Always expect a case

You arrive with only a business problem and have to leave with a technical strategy you can defend. Golden rule: ask what the business wants to change before suggesting any solution.

🧩 Business case

AI plays a customer with a vague problem. You investigate, propose, and defend your solution.

📁 Your proof project

Tell the story as if the interviewer were the customer: the problem, the number, the proof, and what went wrong.

🔧 Technical round

Find a bug in existing code using AI tools, and design a system around data, access, and risks.

🗣️ Behavioral round

An unhappy customer, a request that grew, a mistake you made, saying no. Always explain the situation, your action, and the result in numbers.

The kit includes a ready-to-use prompt for each round: paste it into AI and practice as many times as you want.

Before you get started

Do I need to know how to code?

You need to work with code, but you don’t have to type everything by hand: AI writes it, you read it, ask for the right change, and prove it with a test. If this is your weaker half, the test will show you, and the plan starts there.

Is this role just a passing fad?

No one knows what will happen to a job title. But the work (finding the real problem, building, and proving it) takes more than one skill: it’s the whole cycle, and companies that bought AI are missing it.

How much does the kit cost?

Nothing. The kit and this guide are free. The sample eval runs on your computer, offline and at no cost. What if I don’t want to work for a company abroad?

How long does it take?

The test takes 3 minutes. In our estimate, the learning path and a complete proof project take 1 to 2 months alongside your job, depending on how much work remains in your weaker half.

Do I need a customer to get started?

Not to practice: the simulated customer and the kit’s eval are enough. For the proof project, yes, but it could be your own team, a friend, or a local business.
